On Photography by Susan Sontag

Susan Sontag’s chapter in On Photography, titled “In Plato’s Cave,” seems to bring up photography and picture-taking in a light that urges us to look more closely into our relationship with images. While Sontag paints a distrustful lens onto portraits, she points out that what an individual sees in a photograph may not always be the truth and I agree with this stance. When it comes to capturing images and creating graphics, it is true that the creator or the one capturing the illustration—is responsible for choosing and presenting what they wish to photograph through their very own perspective of the environment/situation. Therefore, I believe image making and photography is fragile, because the slightest frame or misclick can alter how you wish to interpret a photo if done incorrectly. Likewise, this also incites a beauty in photography because it indicates that mistakes happen, and sometimes those misclicks are worth celebrating because they created a graphic that exceeded your original expectations. When it comes to how I use photography myself, I often warp it for social media which is my primary motivation for creating graphics. By posting the smallest selfie or unimportant picture of my lunch, I think I can represent a lot of notions talked about by Sontag, especially when it comes to changing reality. Admittedly, I’ve probably taken tens of photos of a subject only to redo it again and again until I feel it is “just right” which can act as a direct example of Sontag’s analysis; “In deciding how a picture should look, in preferring one exposure to another, photographers are always imposing standards on their subjects.” (pp. 6) So visually, even in our current moment, I survey my pictures religiously and judge environments based on whether they can help my intention or not. And what I mean by intention; is seeing if these images can alter my outward appearance, or how I am perceived by someone else. For example, by taking pictures of scenery constantly or posting stories of myself being productive doing homework, going out for a walk, etc. I can create the impression that I am someone who lives an active or eventful life. Doing things this way, this also means everything I choose to post or capture is selective, and sometimes I even delete every single post I’ve made to “rebrand” myself in another light. Referring back to Sontag, “Photography has become one of the principal devices for experiencing something, for giving an appearance of participation.” (pp. 10) Before I read this statement I was skeptical if it applied to me as well, but it definitely does now that I’ve elaborated on the way I use photography. I can’t say that for every image I’ve taken on vacation or on a walk it was solely for participation, but I’ve definitely decided to keep an image here and there as a reminder of my encounter. With that being said, there is also a contrast I wanted to bring up regarding audiences and their photographers, because I don’t inherently think all realities of photographs can be untrue; some can be real. Especially with photo work surrounding aesthetics, restaurant menus, even inanimate objects, there is possibility for exception and sometimes there are pictures out there meant to encourage instead of raise questions.
